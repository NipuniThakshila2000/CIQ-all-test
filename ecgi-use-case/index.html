Economic Crime and Geopolitics Index

The Economic Crime and Geopolitics Index (ECGI) is a composite analytical framework designed to identify national risk where governance failures, economic crime, public response, and geopolitical pressure converge.

Supported by Conscience IQ and built on Mithra technology, ECGI goes beyond traditional corruption measures by combining multiple indicators into a broader view of instability. Using the Corruption Perceptions Index as its foundational pillar, it adds deeper analysis of economic crime severity, societal response, and geopolitical influence.

This Is Pre-Decision Moral Foresight

Civic Conscience Sandbox

A government, NGO, company, ministry, or public institution can place a draft policy, speech, campaign, law, reform, or public message into the sandbox before launch. The AI then tests how that proposal may be interpreted by different evidence-weighted ECC Profiles.

  • Will this build trust, or trigger mistrust?
  • Which communities may feel unheard, misrepresented, or violated?
  • Where are dignity, religious conscience, gender, shame, honour, safety, justice, or identity sensitivities?
  • How can the language be adjusted before harm is caused?
  • What kind of dialogue is needed before implementation?

The Sandbox is a listening architecture and rehearsal space where institutions can test decisions, simulate stakeholder responses, anticipate consequences, and refine policy before it becomes conflict.

ECC Agents

An ECC Agent is an AI-assisted model of a community's civic conscience, built from consented, structured, revisable community input and an evidence-weighted measurement layer.

It does not speak finally for a community or replace consultation. It gives a pre-dialogue signal so institutions can prepare to listen better.

ECC Agents do not replace communities. They help institutions prepare to listen better before real consultation begins.

CCC Orchestrator

The CCC Orchestrator compares multiple ECC Agents and identifies shared civic values, tensions, translation points, conflict risks, dialogue priorities, and policy redesign options.

Its central question is: where can communities remain distinct and still belong to one civic order?

The CCC Orchestrator helps identify where moral pluralism can be converted into common civic trust rather than fragmentation.

The Challenge

When Institutions Misread Community Conscience

A policy may be technically correct but fail because it ignores dignity, modesty, religious conscience, historical memory, gender sensitivity, institutional mistrust, or local concepts of fairness.

When institutions ignore how communities understand dignity, shame, honour, safety, fairness, justice, corruption, and trust, they create epistemic injustice.

Epistemic injustice occurs when a community's knowledge, testimony, experience, or moral language is dismissed, misunderstood, or excluded from decision-making.

The CIQ Civic Conscience Framework

From Community Conscience to Common Civic Trust

CIQ generates an evidence-weighted ECC profile using multiple validated moral, ethical, social, and political-psychology instruments, then uses that profile to run multi-stakeholder scenario simulations.

ECC Profile

An Epistemic Community Conscience Profile is a structured, evidence-weighted representation of how a community, culture, institution, faith group, profession, or civic population tends to understand dignity, justice, trust, authority, threat, and belonging.

The profile is drawn from validated frameworks and domains rather than claiming to read minds or diagnose individuals. The measurement layer may include moral foundations, moral development, values hierarchy, justice orientation, authority and loyalty orientation, institutional trust, social-dominance and authoritarian tendencies, pluralism tolerance, grievance and identity-threat sensitivity, and reconciliation readiness.

  • What does this community experience as dignity?
  • What does it experience as violation?
  • What does it regard as fair or unjust?
  • Who does it trust as a source of knowledge?
  • What institutional actions feel unsafe, dishonouring, or exclusionary?
  • What conditions are necessary for participation, trust, and belonging?

Red-Line Mapper

CIQ surfaces the conditions where a proposal may be experienced as insult, betrayal, injustice, exclusion, or sacred-value breach. This is not a veto machine. It is a pre-dialogue signal that helps institutions see where language, process, timing, or consultation design may need repair.

CCC Index

The Common Civic Conscience Index is a multi-dimensional dashboard of civic overlap, moral tension, translation capacity, trust gaps, and repair options. It is not a single civic score, not a social-credit mechanism, and not a tool for ranking communities as superior or inferior.

  • Where communities already share civic values
  • Where moral language differs but can be translated
  • Where trust has broken down
  • Where institutional design is creating exclusion
  • Where justice claims overlap or conflict
  • Where dialogue, education, transparency, or process reform is needed

Example

Policy Sensitivity Begins With Moral Context

An epistemically just system asks what a community understands as dignity, modesty, safety, trust, and appropriate care before labeling behavior as non-compliance.

01

Gender-Sensitive Care

In some contexts, women may avoid clinics where male doctors or male nurses are predominant. A purely administrative system may call this low compliance, poor awareness, or lack of access. CIQ asks which dignity, modesty, safety, and trust conditions must exist for care to be accepted.

02

Religious Conscience

During the COVID-19 pandemic, Sri Lanka's mandatory cremation policy created deep distress among Muslim communities whose religious conscience required burial practices. The issue became a question of dignity, trust, religious recognition, and whether affected communities felt heard by the state.

How CIQ AI Works

Aggregate Civic Listening, Not Individual Diagnosis

Conscience IQ AI uses structured questionnaires, guided conversational AI, community-informed prompts, validated domain frameworks, and institutional data layers to generate aggregate civic insight.

The purpose is not individual diagnosis. The purpose is civic listening. Outputs are designed at aggregate level for communities, institutions, departments, or civic groups.

Synthetic modelling is a starting layer for rehearsal, not proof. CIQ becomes defensible as profiles are calibrated against expert judgment, stakeholder data, consultation evidence, and real-world outcomes.
